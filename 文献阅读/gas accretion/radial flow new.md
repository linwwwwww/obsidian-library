## Accretion-driven turbulence as universal process: galaxies, molecular clouds, and protostellar disks 


data: the Milky Way, along with 11 galaxies from the THINGS survey
process:

conclusion:
1. the turbulence at outer part of galaxies is driven by accretion(due to the star formation is low), infall motion is converted to turbulence.
2. To drive the observed level of turbulence, the accretion rate needs to exceed the star formation rate,
other:
It is interesting in this context that the transition from the star-forming parts of the galaxy to the non-star-forming outer disk does not seem to cause significant changes in the velocity dispersion (Tamburro et al. 2009)
Roughly three quarters of all star-forming galaxies are fed by smooth streams (see also Agertz et al. 2009)
Here accretion driven turbulence seems a viable option (see, e.g.
Santillán et al. 2007).
